## Round 0 — DEFENDER

The Pathogen Control (PC) hypothesis posits that aging is not a byproduct of neglected repair, but a programmed, adaptive response shaped by the requirements of epidemic suppression. While Classic Models view aging as an inevitable result of declining selection pressure and resource allocation tradeoffs (Disposable Soma), PC argues that lifespan setpoints are actively selected to minimize pathogen persistence and spread within a population. 

### Theoretical Framework

1. **Ultimate (Evolutionary) Claim**: Lifespan is an adaptive setpoint selected to curb pathogen persistence and spread within a population [@LidskyAndino2020]. 
   - *Bridge Assumption*: Population-level epidemic suppression provides a selective advantage (e.g., through kin selection or group stability) that can favor programmed death over individual-level longevity.

2. **Proximate (Mechanistic) Claim**: Cellular senescence functions as an antiviral 'immune militia,' where the accumulation of these cells provides a barrier to pathogen replication [@LidskyAndino2022].
   - *Bridge Assumption*: The inflammatory and growth-arrested state of senescent cells is specifically evolved for viral interference, and their 'aging' effects are a necessary cost of this defense.

3. **Ultimate (Evolutionary) Claim**: Species-level lifespan variation is driven by population structure (dispersal, density, and mixing patterns) [@LidskyAndino2022].
   - *Bridge Assumption*: High dispersal rates (e.g., in birds and bats) break transmission chains, reducing the selective pressure for short lifespans.

4. **Measurement-level (Dynamics) Claim**: Longevity interventions will show a measurable tradeoff with infection resistance in pathogen-rich environments [@LidskyAndino2022].
   - *Bridge Assumption*: SPF (Specific Pathogen Free) laboratory conditions mask the adaptive penalties of longevity, leading to an overestimation of 'cost-free' rejuvenation.

### Key Points of the Case

- **Epidemic Curbing**: Programmed death prevents the 'carryover' of pathogens between cohorts, effectively 'resetting' the population's infection status [@LidskyAndino2020].
- **Senescence as Defense**: Cellular senescence is not just damage; it is a coordinated antiviral response. Clearing it (as in senolytics) should, in theory, impair certain immune responses under challenge [@LidskyAndino2022].
- **Environmental Dependency**: Longevity is only 'safe' in environments with low pathogen burden or high dispersal. This explains why flyers (birds/bats) and eusocial species (where queens are isolated) exhibit unusual longevity [@LidskyAndino2022].
- **Tradeoff Logic**: Unlike the resource-based tradeoffs of the Disposable Soma theory, PC predicts specifically *infection-based* tradeoffs for lifespan extension.

**Key Points:**
- Aging is an adaptive, programmed response selected to curb pathogen persistence and spread (Ultimate).
- Lifespan setpoints are determined by population structure and epidemic dynamics (Ultimate).
- Cellular senescence serves as an antiviral 'immune militia' (Proximate).
- Population structure (e.g., dispersal in birds/bats) dictates the selective cost of longevity (Ultimate).
- Longevity gains in pathogen-rich environments incur specific infection costs unless paired with anti-pathogen measures (Proximate/Measurement).

**Falsifiers:**
- Significant lifespan extension (e.g., >20%) in 'dirty' or wild-exposed environments that does not increase pathogen burden or transmission.
- Demonstration that senescent cells provide no protective benefit against any class of natural pathogens in vivo.
- Absence of correlation between dispersal/population mixing and maximum lifespan when controlling for body size and phylogeny.
- Interventions that extend lifespan while improving pathogen clearance in high-burden settings without specific anti-pathogen additives.

**Proposed Experiments:**
- The 'Dirty Mouse' Rejuvenation Challenge: Perform partial reprogramming (OSK) or stacked repairs in mice housed in naturalistic 'dirty' environments vs. SPF. PC predicts that gains in SPF will be significantly attenuated or flipped to net mortality in dirty environments due to infection susceptibility.
- Senolytic Infection Tradeoff: Administer senolytics to mice during a controlled viral challenge. PC predicts increased viral titers and/or transmission compared to untreated aged controls.
- Pathogen-Induced Lifespan Reset: Test if deliberate pathogen clearance (e.g., aggressive antiviral/antibiotic stacks) in aged cohorts resets the hazard rate or autocorrelation time (resilience) more effectively than intrinsic repair mechanisms.

## Round 1 — CRITIC

## Claim Typing and Bridge Assumptions (Pathogen Control)

1. **Ultimate (evolutionary)**: Lifespan is an adaptive setpoint selected to reduce pathogen persistence/spread.
Bridge assumptions: population-level epidemic suppression yields inclusive-fitness gains (e.g., via kin selection / group stability) that outweigh individual-level selection for survival; selectable heritable lifespan control exists in realistic population structures.

2. **Proximate (mechanistic)**: Cellular senescence is an evolved antiviral barrier ('immune militia'); its accumulation materially limits pathogen replication.
Bridge assumptions: senescence provides a net antiviral benefit in vivo that is hard to achieve more cheaply; organism-level aging phenotypes are a necessary cost of this defense; clearing senescent cells should impair host defense under relevant challenges.

3. **Ultimate (evolutionary)**: Species lifespan variation is primarily driven by dispersal/density/mixing patterns that change epidemic dynamics.
Bridge assumptions: these population-structure variables explain lifespan beyond standard life-history covariates; correlations persist under body-size and phylogenetic controls.

4. **Measurement-level (dynamics/endpoints)**: Longevity interventions will show infection-specific tradeoffs in pathogen-rich environments; SPF housing masks penalties.
Bridge assumptions: lifespan extension disrupts an evolved pathogen-control regime (not just general physiology or drug toxicity); infection endpoints in dirty/rewilded settings reflect the historically relevant selection pressures.

## Classic Models Attack

Classic models explain aging as a byproduct of (a) declining force of selection with age and antagonistic pleiotropy and (b) finite investment in maintenance/repair (disposable soma). On this view, pathogens are one of many ecological pressures shaping allocation, but no adaptive lifespan setpoint or programmed death is required.

1. **Ultimate-level plausibility gap**: PC's core ultimate claim hinges on strong population-level bridges (kin/group benefits large enough to favor shortened lifespan) that are not established by the current in-repo evidence base (largely modeling/review). Classic models generate senescence without invoking selection for population-wide epidemic suppression.

2. **Non-uniqueness vs allocation tradeoffs**: PC's 'infection tradeoffs' can be recast as ordinary maintenance-allocation or intervention-toxicity effects (the repo's own examples include dose/context dependence of mTOR inhibition on infections). Without a sharper prediction that distinguishes infection-specific constraints from generic tradeoffs, PC does not outperform Classics.

3. **Senescence mechanism underdetermined**: The proximate claim that senescence is primarily an antiviral militia is not supported by direct in vivo pathogen-challenge endpoints in this repo, and it sits uneasily with evidence that senescent-cell clearance delays pathologies and senolytics can increase lifespan. Absent challenge studies, the parsimonious reading under Classics is that senescence accumulates because late-life maintenance is imperfect, with any antimicrobial effects being incidental or context-limited.

4. **Precision problem at the measurement level**: PC can accommodate both 'longevity increases infections' and 'longevity improves immunity' by appealing to environment and dose, which makes the signature less falsifiable unless endpoints and regimes are specified in advance (pathogen identity, inoculum, transmission, and survival/hazard analysis).

5. **Population-structure claims need controlled tests**: PC itself lists 'no correlation after controlling for body size and phylogeny' as a falsifier. Until those analyses are done, structure-based anecdotes are suggestive but not diagnostic; Classics expect many correlated ecological variables to co-vary with lifespan.

## What Is Missing (Decisive for This Dispute)

1. Pre-registered lifespan studies in dirty/rewilded conditions with pathogen burden and transmission endpoints.
2. Direct tests of whether senescent-cell clearance causally worsens control of natural pathogens in vivo.
3. Phylogenetically controlled comparative analyses quantifying whether dispersal/mixing predicts lifespan beyond standard covariates.

**Key Points:**
- PC’s ultimate claim depends on strong kin/group-selection bridges that are not yet established in the repo’s evidence (mostly modeling/review), while Classic Models explain aging without adaptive death.
- PC’s proximate senescence-as-antiviral claim lacks direct in vivo pathogen-challenge support here and is at least as consistent with a maintenance-failure interpretation given senescence-clearance benefits.
- Infection tradeoffs of longevity interventions are not uniquely diagnostic: dose/context and generic allocation/toxicity mechanisms can yield either increased or decreased infection risk.
- Population-structure explanations require phylogenetically controlled, quantitative tests to separate PC from confounded life-history correlations.

**Criticisms:**
- Ultimate-level: PC requires population-level (kin/group) benefits large enough to outweigh individual-level selection for survival; this bridge assumption is the main plausibility gap versus classic selection-gradient accounts.
- The in-repo support for PC is primarily theoretical modeling and perspective/review, not empirical demonstrations of evolved lifespan setpoints under pathogen-driven selection.
- Ultimate-level: PC’s population-structure explanation is not yet backed by decisive phylogenetically controlled analyses in this repo; correlations could be confounded by standard life-history variables.
- Proximate-level: senescence-as-antiviral-militia is under-supported here; evidence that senescent-cell clearance delays pathology and senolytics can extend lifespan fits a damage/maintenance-failure story at least as well.
- Measurement-level: the predicted infection tradeoff of longevity interventions is not uniquely informative because context/dose and intervention toxicity can explain infection outcomes without invoking an evolved lifespan setpoint.
- PC risks post hoc flexibility (SPF vs dirty, dose/context) unless it pre-specifies pathogens, transmission metrics, and hazard endpoints that distinguish infection-specific constraints from generic tradeoffs.

**Falsifiers:**
- Across multiple independent, pre-registered dirty/rewilded lifespan studies, diverse lifespan-extending interventions consistently increase pathogen burden and transmission (not just incidental infections) in a way rescued specifically by targeted anti-pathogen measures, without mediation by reproduction/energy-allocation changes.
- Genetic or pharmacologic senescent-cell clearance reproducibly worsens control of natural pathogens in vivo (higher titers/shedding/transmission and mortality) with confounds from drug immunosuppression ruled out, and the effect is rescued by restoring senescence-linked antiviral factors.
- Phylogenetically controlled comparative analyses show dispersal/mixing variables explain substantial maximum-lifespan variance beyond body size and standard covariates, matching PC’s predicted direction and magnitude.
- Large lifespan gains observed under SPF conditions systematically fail (or reverse) under dirty/rewilded exposure across labs unless anti-pathogen interventions co-apply, indicating pathogen ecology is the primary constraint rather than generic maintenance tradeoffs.

**Proposed Experiments:**
- Dirty/rewilded factorial lifespan test: SPF vs dirty/rewilded housing crossed with a longevity intervention (e.g., senolytics as in Xu2018 or p16Ink4a+ cell clearance as in Baker2011) and with/without an anti-pathogen package (vaccination/antivirals). Endpoints: median/max lifespan, pathogen burden, and transmission proxies.
- Senescence antiviral-barrier challenge: aged mice with senescent-cell clearance (genetic or senolytic) vs controls under standardized viral challenge; pre-register viral titers, shedding/transmission, survival, and immunopathology; include timing arms (clearance before vs after infection).
- Comparative phylogenetic model test: compile multi-species dataset of maximum lifespan and quantitative dispersal/mixing metrics; pre-register phylogenetic regressions controlling for body size and life-history covariates; test incremental predictive value of structure variables.
- Allocation mediation under pathogen exposure: under controlled infection pressure, manipulate maintenance budget (energy availability and/or reproduction opportunity) alongside a lifespan-extending intervention; test whether any infection penalty tracks allocation shifts (Classic Models) versus persisting when allocation is held constant (supports PC).

## Round 2 — DEFENDER

## Response to Criticisms

### 1. Ultimate-level Plausibility (Selection Bridges)
The criticism that Pathogen Control (PC) relies on weak kin-selection bridges ignores the mathematical robustness of inclusive fitness in spatially structured populations. Modeling in @LidskyAndino2020 demonstrates that reducing local pathogen persistence through finite lifespan can be a stable evolutionary strategy. Unlike Classic Models, which treat pathogens as a generic extrinsic mortality factor, PC predicts that **population structure** (mixing/dispersal) determines the specific lifespan setpoint. 
- **Bridge Assumption**: Inclusive fitness gains from epidemic suppression in kin-structured groups outweigh individual selection for survival.

### 2. Senescence as Antiviral Militia (Proximate Bridge)
The argument that senolytic benefits in SPF mice disprove the PC militia hypothesis ignores environmental context. In SPF facilities, the "militia" (senescent cells) is mobilized against non-existent threats, appearing only as deleterious "inflammaging" (@Franceschi2018). PC predicts that in naturalistic exposure models (@nature19801), senescent cell clearance will increase pathogen titers and transmission.
- **Bridge Assumption**: Senescence provides a net antiviral benefit in vivo that is required only under natural pathogen challenge.

### 3. Non-Uniqueness vs. Allocation Tradeoffs
Classics argue that infection tradeoffs are merely resource allocation shifts. PC provides a unique prediction: if the tradeoff is driven by pathogen dynamics (setpoint), then adding **anti-pathogen interventions** (vaccines/antivirals) should rescue the lifespan of "long-lived" variants without requiring additional energy/reproduction shifts. 
- **Bridge Assumption**: Lifespan extension disrupts a specific evolved pathogen-control regime rather than a generic metabolic budget.

### 4. Empirical Base and Comparative Tests
We acknowledge the need for phylogenetically controlled tests. We propose using rewilded mouse models (@science.aau5145) to test if lifespan extension (e.g., via mTOR inhibition or OSK) consistently increases transmission of natural pathogens (e.g., MHV, MNV) compared to SPF controls. This distinguishes PC from Classics, which would expect the same relative gains regardless of pathogen pressure if resources are held constant.

**Key Points:**
- Adaptive Lifespan Setpoint (Ultimate): Lifespan is selected to limit pathogen persistence; shorter lifespans evolve where transmission costs to inclusive fitness are high (@LidskyAndino2020).
- Population Structure Determinism (Ultimate): Dispersal and mixing patterns drive lifespan evolution by altering epidemic thresholds (@LidskyAndino2022).
- Senescence as Immune Militia (Proximate): Senescent cells function as an antiviral barrier; their accumulation is an evolved defense that becomes pathological primarily in the absence of pathogens (SPF) (@LidskyAndino2022).
- Environment-Dependent Tradeoffs (Measurement): Longevity gains will consistently show infection costs in naturalistic settings that are masked in SPF facilities (@nature19801, @science.aau5145).
- Pathogen-Specific Rescue: Anti-pathogen measures can decouple longevity from its fitness costs without adjusting reproduction or somatic maintenance budgets, a result not predicted by resource-allocation models (Classics).

**Criticisms:**
- Ultimate-level plausibility gap: PC requires population-level (kin/group) benefits large enough to outweigh individual-level selection for survival.
- The in-repo support for PC is primarily theoretical modeling and perspective/review, not empirical demonstrations of evolved lifespan setpoints.
- Population-structure claims need phylogenetically controlled, quantitative tests to separate PC from confounded life-history correlations.
- Senescence-as-antiviral-militia is under-supported; evidence that senescent-cell clearance delays pathology fits a damage/maintenance-failure story.
- Infection tradeoffs of longevity interventions are not uniquely informative because context/dose and intervention toxicity can explain outcomes.
- PC risks post hoc flexibility (SPF vs dirty, dose/context) unless it pre-specifies pathogens and hazard endpoints.

**Falsifiers:**
- Longevity interventions (OSK, senolytics, mTORi) extend lifespan in 'dirty/rewilded' environments WITHOUT increasing pathogen titers or transmission rates relative to controls.
- Targeted senescent cell clearance (e.g., p16-DTA) in a naturalistic/dirty environment improves resistance to viral challenge (lower titers/transmission) compared to non-cleared controls.
- Comparative phylogenetic analysis shows that dispersal/mixing metrics provide no additional predictive power for maximum lifespan once body size and standard ecological covariates (extrinsic mortality) are controlled for.
- Anti-pathogen rescues (vaccination/antivirals) fail to restore the lifespan of long-lived variants under infection pressure, indicating the tradeoff is a fixed somatic budget (Classics) rather than a pathogen-control setpoint (PC).

**Proposed Experiments:**
- Factorial Rewilding Study: SPF vs. Dirty housing x Longevity Intervention (mTORi/OSK) x Anti-pathogen rescue (Vaccine/Antiviral). Measure lifespan, pathogen burden, and energy expenditure.
- Senescence Challenge in Dirty Mice: Aged mice with/without p16+ cell clearance (genetic) housed in 'dirty' conditions (@nature19801). Endpoint: Survival and transmission rates of endemic pathogens.
- Phylogenetic Dispersal Analysis: Multi-species regression of maximum lifespan against dispersal distance and group mixing frequency, controlling for phylogeny, body mass, and habitat-specific extrinsic mortality.
- Transmission-Hazard Endpoint Test: Pre-registered study measuring if longevity-extending treatments increase the 'Basic Reproduction Number' (R0) of a standardized viral inoculum in a cohort, independent of individual survival.

## Claim Typing (Pathogen Control) and Bridge Assumptions

## Judge Verdict: Pathogen Control (Defender) vs Classic Models (Critic)

**Winner: Critic (Classic Models)**

The Critic wins on Popperian grounds — not because Classic Models are proven correct, but because the Critic successfully demonstrated that Pathogen Control's defended claims rest on bridge assumptions that remain untested within this repo's evidence base, while Classic Models' core mechanism (the selection shadow) is a mathematical consequence requiring fewer auxiliary assumptions. The Defender's case was coherent and generated sharper predictions than Classics, but failed to close the gap between theoretical modeling and empirical demonstration that the Critic repeatedly and correctly identified.

## Top 3 Surviving Criticisms (Critic Prevails)

1. **Ultimate-level: PC's empirical base is modeling + review only.** The Critic's most persistent and damaging point. The in-repo evidence for PC consists entirely of theoretical modeling (@LidskyAndino2020, PNAS) and perspective/review articles (@LidskyAndino2022, TREE; @LidskyYuanAndino2023; @LidskyEtAl2022). The evidence.csv entries for lidsky are both marked as 'theory' or 'review' with no empirical species data. By contrast, Classic Models rest on decades of empirical work across taxa. Under Popperian criteria, a theory that has not yet been severely tested cannot claim to have survived such tests, regardless of how testable it is in principle. PC scores severe_tests=1 in its own frontmatter — the Critic correctly leveraged this.

2. **Proximate-level: Senescence-as-antiviral-militia is underdetermined by available evidence.** The Critic demonstrated that senescent-cell clearance data (@Baker2011, @Xu2018) fit a damage/maintenance-failure interpretation at least as well as an antiviral-militia one. The Defender's counter — that SPF conditions remove the relevant pathogen challenge — is logically consistent but shifts the burden to future experiments rather than resolving the ambiguity. Without in vivo pathogen-challenge data showing senescent-cell clearance worsens viral control, this proximate claim remains a hypothesis, not a supported mechanism.

3. **Measurement-level: Infection tradeoffs are not uniquely diagnostic without pre-specification.** The Critic identified that PC can accommodate both 'longevity increases infections' and 'longevity improves immunity' by appealing to environment, dose, and context. The mTOR inhibitor literature (transplant-dose immunosuppression vs low-dose immune enhancement) illustrates this flexibility. Unless PC pre-registers specific pathogens, exposure regimes, and transmission endpoints, its predictions are less risky than they appear. The Defender's proposed experiments would address this — but they remain proposed, not executed.

## Top 3 Strongest Defenses (Defender's Best Arguments)

1. **Novel, specific predictions that Classics cannot generate.** PC predicts: (a) longevity interventions will show infection penalties under pathogen challenge that are absent in SPF; (b) anti-pathogen co-interventions can rescue longevity without adjusting reproduction/energy budgets; (c) population structure predicts lifespan at constant extrinsic mortality. None of these are generated by Classic Models. The Defender's proposed experimental designs (factorial dirty-mouse studies, phylogenetic dispersal analyses, transmission-hazard endpoints) are well-constructed and genuinely discriminating. PC scores novel_predictions=3 vs Classics' 1 — the Defender correctly highlighted this asymmetry.

2. **Environmental dependency explains SPF-only results.** The Defender's argument that SPF conditions systematically mask PC's predicted tradeoffs is consistent with rewilding literature (@nature19801, @science.aau5145) showing that SPF-to-naturalistic transitions reshape immune tone and can flip intervention effects. This is not a post hoc save — it is a pre-specified prediction that SPF results should not generalize to pathogen-rich conditions. The Critic's counter (that this adds flexibility) is partly valid, but the Defender committed to specific experimental designs that would constrain the flexibility.

3. **Coherent framework linking ultimate and proximate levels.** PC provides a rare complete causal chain: ultimate cause (pathogen persistence selects for finite lifespan) → proximate mechanism (senescence as antiviral militia) → measurement predictions (infection tradeoffs, anti-pathogen rescue, population-structure effects). Most aging theories operate at one level; PC's multi-level architecture is a strength for generating testable bridge assumptions, even though those bridges remain unverified.

## Why the Critic Wins on Popperian Grounds

1. **Severe testing gap.** Popperian evaluation prioritizes theories that have *survived* severe tests, not merely those that *propose* them. PC's proposals are excellent, but the repo records zero completed severe tests for PC (severe_tests=1 in frontmatter, and evidence.csv contains only modeling/review entries). Classic Models also score severe_tests=1, but their foundational predictions (Gompertzian mortality curves, extrinsic-mortality correlates of lifespan, reproduction-longevity tradeoffs) have been tested across taxa for decades. The Defender did not dispute this empirical asymmetry.

2. **Unearned bridge assumptions.** The Critic systematically identified that PC's claim structure depends on bridges (inclusive fitness outweighs individual selection; senescence is net antiviral in vivo; dispersal predicts lifespan beyond covariates) that are each individually untested. The Defender acknowledged the gaps and proposed experiments — but acknowledgment is not evidence. Under Popperian criteria, a theory whose bridge assumptions are untested cannot claim empirical content beyond its modeling.

3. **Post hoc flexibility at the measurement level.** The Critic's precision critique is valid: PC can explain both positive and negative infection outcomes by invoking SPF vs dirty, dose/context, and timing. Without pre-registration, this flexibility reduces PC's effective falsifiability below its stated level. The Defender's proposed experiments would fix this — but they are proposals, not results.

4. **Classic Models' mathematical foundation.** The selection shadow (@Hamilton1966) is not a hypothesis — it is a mathematical consequence of non-zero extrinsic hazard. PC must operate *in addition to* this foundation, adding explanatory burden. The Defender implicitly accepted this by framing PC as extending, not replacing, classic evolutionary theory. This means PC's unique contribution must be demonstrated empirically to justify the additional theoretical machinery.

## Important Caveat: PC's Potential

This verdict should not be read as a dismissal of Pathogen Control. PC is the more *interesting* theory by Popperian lights — it generates novel predictions, commits to specific falsifiers, and proposes genuinely discriminating experiments. It has higher falsifiability (3 vs 3), riskiness (3 vs 2), and novel predictions (3 vs 1) than Classic Models in the repo's own scoring. The Critic wins because the Defender could not overcome the simple fact that PC's predictions remain untested. If the proposed factorial dirty-mouse studies, phylogenetic analyses, and transmission-endpoint experiments are executed, PC could rapidly accumulate empirical content and overtake Classics in Popperian standing. The current verdict is a snapshot, not a permanent ranking.

## Decisive Next Tests

1. **Pre-registered factorial longevity × exposure × pathogen control (highest priority).** Apply a strong longevity intervention (rapamycin, senolytics, or partial reprogramming) in a 2×2×2 design: (SPF vs dirty/rewilded) × (intervention vs control) × (anti-pathogen package vs none). Pre-register specific pathogens to track (e.g., MHV, MNV, Helicobacter). Primary endpoints: maximum lifespan, mortality hazard, pathogen burden/persistence, shedding duration, and transmission to sentinel cohoused mice. Secondary: reproduction, energy expenditure, body composition. PC predicts infection penalties in dirty housing rescued by anti-pathogen co-intervention; Classics predict generic metabolic tradeoffs present even in SPF. This single study could shift the verdict.

2. **Senescence antiviral-barrier challenge.** Aged mice with genetic p16Ink4a+ senescent-cell clearance (@Baker2011) vs controls under standardized viral challenge in dirty/rewilded conditions. Pre-register viral titers, shedding, transmission to sentinels, survival, and immunopathology. Include timing arms (clearance before vs after infection) and a rescue arm restoring candidate senescence-linked antiviral factors. PC predicts clearance worsens pathogen control; Classics predict clearance improves or does not change outcomes.

3. **Phylogenetically controlled comparative analysis.** Compile a multi-species dataset of maximum lifespan with quantitative dispersal/mixing metrics. Pre-register phylogenetic regressions controlling for body mass and standard life-history covariates (extrinsic mortality, metabolic rate, reproductive rate). Test incremental predictive value of population-structure variables and out-of-sample prediction accuracy. PC predicts substantial incremental variance explained by structure; Classics predict structure adds nothing beyond extrinsic mortality.

4. **Experimental evolution under controlled mixing and infection.** Replicate populations (e.g., Drosophila or C. elegans) with matched total extrinsic mortality and resources but different mixing regimes (high dispersal vs strict cohorts), crossed with chronic pathogen exposure. Track evolved lifespan, pathogen persistence, and reproduction over 50+ generations. PC predicts structure and pathogen pressure shift evolved lifespan; Classics predict no shift at constant extrinsic mortality.

- No germ-free/gnotobiotic lifespan data (relevant to both theories).
- Both theories score severe_tests=1, confirming neither has passed stringent discriminating tests in this repo.

**Summary:**

- PC's ultimate claim (adaptive lifespan setpoint for epidemic suppression) relies on inclusive-fitness bridges that are theoretically plausible (@LidskyAndino2020) but empirically untested in this repo; the Critic correctly and persistently identified this as the central gap.

- The decisive next test is a pre-registered 2×2×2 factorial (longevity intervention × environment × anti-pathogen package) with infection-ecology primary endpoints — this single study could shift the verdict.
- Both theories score severe_tests=1; the field needs pathogen-challenge factorial designs, phylogenetic structure analyses, and senescence-challenge experiments to discriminate between them.
