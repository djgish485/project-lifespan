## Round 0 — DEFENDER

### Opening Steelman: Classic Models of Aging

Classic Models (Medawar, Williams, Hamilton, Kirkwood) posit that aging is an emergent byproduct of evolutionary mechanics, not a programmed adaptation. It arises from the inescapable decline in the force of natural selection with age.

#### 1. Ultimate Level: The Evolution of Senescence
Aging evolves because the pressure to maintain the organism weakens after the peak reproductive period.
*   **Mutation Accumulation (Medawar 1952)**: Late-acting deleterious mutations accumulate because they are "invisible" to selection in a wild environment where most individuals die from extrinsic causes (predation, accidents) before these mutations manifest.
*   **Antagonistic Pleiotropy (Williams 1957)**: Selection actively favors genes that enhance early-life fitness even if they cause senescence later, as the early benefit carries more selective weight.
*   **Bridge Assumption**: **The Selection Shadow.** The strength of natural selection on an individual's fitness contribution is proportional to the probability of that individual being alive and reproductive at that age in the wild.

#### 2. Proximate Level: The Disposable Soma
The mechanism of aging is the accumulation of somatic damage due to evolved priorities in resource allocation.
*   **Disposable Soma (Kirkwood 1977)**: Organisms allocate limited metabolic resources between reproduction and somatic maintenance. To maximize fitness, the soma is maintained only well enough to reach successful reproduction, after which it is "disposable."
*   **Bridge Assumption**: **Metabolic Tradeoffs.** Somatic repair (DNA repair, proteostasis) is energetically costly and subject to diminishing returns; therefore, perfect repair is never evolutionarily optimal.

#### 3. Measurement Level: Interventions and Costs
Empirical observations of aging should reflect these fundamental tradeoffs.
*   **Tradeoff Inevitability**: Any intervention that significantly extends maximum lifespan (>= 15%) must incur a "fitness tax," such as reduced fertility, increased cancer risk, or impaired resilience to environmental stress (classics-p1).
*   **Extrinsic Mortality Primacy**: Cross-species variation in lifespan is primarily driven by the rate of extrinsic mortality. Species with lower extrinsic hazard (e.g., flyers, armored species) evolve longer lifespans because their selection shadow is pushed further back.
*   **Bridge Assumption**: **Fitness Decoupling.** While healthspan and lifespan can be partially uncoupled in protected environments, the underlying tradeoffs remain latent and measurable under physiological stress.

#### Contrast with Pathogen Control (Lidsky)
Classics contend that Lidsky's population-structure patterns are better explained by extrinsic mortality. High dispersal (birds/bats) reduces predation and accidental death, which is the primary driver for the evolution of longevity. Lidsky's theory requires an adaptive "program" for death; Classics require only the absence of selection for indefinite maintenance.

**Key Points:**
- Aging is a non-adaptive byproduct of the declining force of natural selection with age (Medawar 1952, Hamilton 1966).
- Selection favors genes with early-life fitness benefits even if they cause late-life decline (Antagonistic Pleiotropy, Williams 1957).
- Aging results from finite resource investment in somatic repair vs. reproduction (Disposable Soma, Kirkwood 1977).
- Longevity is primarily determined by the rate of extrinsic mortality (predation, accidents) in the wild.
- Significant lifespan gains (>15%) are constrained by fundamental fitness tradeoffs (classics-p1).

**Falsifiers:**
- A longevity intervention that yields >= 15% max lifespan gain in WT mice with no measurable fitness costs (tumors, reproduction, or stress resistance) (classics-p1).
- Population structure variables (dispersal, mixing) provide >= 5% incremental R-squared in predicting lifespan after controlling for extrinsic mortality and allometry (classics-p2).
- Discovery of a 'death program' gene whose sole function is to limit lifespan for group benefit without providing any early-life fitness advantage.

**Proposed Experiments:**
- 2x2x2 Factorial Pathogen Challenge: Test longevity interventions in SPF vs. rewilded environments. Classics predicts resource-based tradeoffs (e.g., cold stress) regardless of pathogen presence, while Lidsky predicts specific infection penalties (docs/experiments/research_agenda.md#1-pathogen-challenge-factorial-2x2x2).
- Global Phylogenetic Allometric Analysis: Re-evaluating mammalian lifespan data to test if population structure variables add predictive power beyond extrinsic mortality rates (classics-p2).

## Round 1 — CRITIC

### Critic's Attack: Pathogen Control vs. Classic Models

Classic Models (Medawar, Williams, Kirkwood) frame aging as a passive failure of selection or a resource-allocation byproduct. Pathogen Control (PC) argues that aging is an active, adaptive strategy shaped by epidemic dynamics.

#### 1. Ultimate Level: The Selection Shadow vs. Inclusive Fitness
Classics rely on the bridge assumption of **The Selection Shadow** (Medawar 1952, Hamilton 1966), where late-life maintenance fails because individuals die of accidents first. PC refutes this passive view, proposing the bridge of **Inclusive Fitness Defense** (@LidskyAndino2020): death is an adaptive program to prevent long-lived individuals from becoming chronic pathogen reservoirs that threaten kin survival. The 'shadow' is not an accidental void but an active boundary for group persistence.

#### 2. Proximate Level: Disposable Soma vs. Antiviral Militia
Classics rely on **Metabolic Tradeoffs** (Kirkwood 1977), viewing cellular senescence as 'damage' resulting from budget constraints. PC proposes the bridge of **Functional Senescence** (@LidskyAndino2022): senescent cells are an evolved **Antiviral Militia** designed to halt viral replication. Rejuvenation (e.g., senolytics) should therefore incur a specific host-defense tax (pc-p1), not just a generic resource tax.

#### 3. Measurement Level: Fitness Decoupling vs. Pathogen Interaction
Classics rely on **Fitness Decoupling** (classics-p1), predicting that longevity extension always has 'some' cost. PC predicts a specific **Pathogen-Environment Interaction** (pc-p1). Lifespan gains observed in SPF (clean) environments do not validate Classics; they reflect the removal of the primary selective pressure (pathogens). True tests require naturalistic/rewilded environments where the adaptive cost of longevity can manifest.

#### 4. The Flyers Paradox
Classics struggle to explain why flyers (birds/bats) have extreme longevity despite high metabolic rates, attributing it solely to low predation. PC identifies population dispersal as a superior predictor: high mobility breaks epidemic persistence, reducing the selective pressure for early death and allowing longevity to evolve (@LidskyAndino2022).

**Criticisms:**
- Failure to explain population-structure-linked lifespan patterns (dispersal, contact density) that track epidemic persistence rather than just extrinsic mortality (S1-CLASSICS-PC-02).
- The 'Shadow of Selection' is not a passive byproduct but an active evolutionary boundary driven by inclusive fitness in the face of pathogens (@LidskyAndino2020).
- Cellular senescence is mischaracterized as 'damage'; PC identifies it as a functional antiviral adaptation (Antiviral Militia) that should show specific tradeoffs under infection challenge (@LidskyAndino2022).
- The generic 'tradeoff' framing of Classics (classics-p1) is insufficient; PC predicts a specific, environment-dependent infection penalty (pc-p1) that is invisible in SPF facilities.

**Falsifiers:**
- If longevity interventions (OSK or senolytics) in 'dirty/rewilded' mice show no increase in pathogen burden or transmission across 3+ pathogen classes (pc-p1).
- If population structure variables (dispersal, cohort mixing) provide < 2% incremental R-squared in predicting mammalian lifespan after controlling for extrinsic mortality and allometry (pc-p2).

**Proposed Experiments:**
- 2x2x2 Factorial Pathogen Challenge: Testing longevity interventions in SPF vs. rewilded environments with pre-registered infection endpoints (docs/experiments/research_agenda.md#1-pathogen-challenge-factorial-2x2x2).
- Global Phylogenetic Comparative Analysis: Testing if population structure variables add predictive power for lifespan across 50+ species beyond standard allometric and extrinsic hazard metrics (pc-p2).

## Round 2 — DEFENDER

### Response to Criticism: The Parity of Selection

Classic Models remain the most parsimonious explanation for aging. The criticisms from Pathogen Control (PC) attempt to rebrand fundamental evolutionary constraints as specialized adaptations. We address these at three levels.

#### 1. Ultimate Level: The Inevitability of the Selection Shadow
Classics rely on the bridge assumption of **The Force of Selection** (Hamilton 1966), which mathematically proves that selection pressure must decline with age in any population with non-zero extrinsic mortality. PC’s **Inclusive Fitness Defense** (@LidskyAndino2020) is an ad hoc addition that is not required to explain the data. For adaptive death to evolve, the benefit to kin (reducing pathogen spread) must exceed the individual’s remaining reproductive value. Since the 'shadow' already removes selection for late-life maintenance, a specialized 'program' for death is a redundant explanation for a void created by basic probability.

#### 2. Proximate Level: Antagonistic Pleiotropy vs. Antiviral Militia
Classics rely on the bridge assumption of **Antagonistic Pleiotropy** (Williams 1957). PC's **Antiviral Militia** (@LidskyAndino2022) is not a rival mechanism but a specific instance of pleiotropy: a trait (senescence) that provides a benefit in youth (pathogen defense, wound healing) is selected for despite its deleterious effects in old age (SASP, inflammation). The 'host-defense tax' PC predicts (pc-p1) is simply a specific case of the **Resource/Fitness Tradeoff** (classics-p1). If senolytics impair host defense, it confirms the pleiotropic value of those cells, not that aging is a program.

#### 3. Measurement Level: Extrinsic Risk vs. Epidemic Persistence
Classics rely on the bridge assumption of **Extrinsic Mortality Hazard** (Medawar 1952). The 'Flyers Paradox' is resolved by the fact that flight drastically reduces predation and accident risk. PC’s claim that dispersal tracks epidemic persistence is likely a correlate: high-mobility species often have lower extrinsic mortality. The decisive test is whether population structure adds predictive power beyond extrinsic hazard (classics-p2).

#### 4. The SPF 'Clean Room' Red Herring
Lifespan gains in SPF environments do not 'fail' to validate Classics; they exemplify the removal of extrinsic constraints that allow the underlying lack of maintenance (disposable soma, Kirkwood 1977) to manifest. If PC were correct, 'rewilded' mice should show a programmed death at a fixed interval; instead, they show variable survival driven by the reappearance of extrinsic stressors and pleiotropic costs (classics-p1).

**Key Points:**
- Aging is an emergent property of the declining force of natural selection with age (Hamilton 1966).
- Cellular senescence is a primary example of Antagonistic Pleiotropy (Williams 1957), where early-life benefits trade off with late-life pathology.
- Population structure correlates with extrinsic mortality; high dispersal reduces predation and accident risk, allowing longer lifespans to evolve (Medawar 1952).
- Substantial lifespan extension without measurable fitness costs (tumors, fertility, or stress resistance) is impossible under resource-limited selection (classics-p1).

**Criticisms:**
- The 'Shadow of Selection' is not a passive byproduct but an active evolutionary boundary driven by inclusive fitness.
- Cellular senescence is mischaracterized as 'damage' rather than a functional antiviral adaptation (Antiviral Militia).
- Failure to explain population-structure-linked lifespan patterns that track epidemic persistence.
- The generic 'tradeoff' framing is insufficient compared to PC's environment-dependent predictions.

**Falsifiers:**
- If a longevity intervention (e.g., OSK or multi-class repair) yields >= 15% maximum lifespan gain in C57BL/6J mice with NO measurable fitness cost (tumor delta <= 5%, reproduction decline <= 10%, stress resistance decline <= 15%) (classics-p1).
- If population structure variables (dispersal, contact density) provide >= 5% incremental R-squared in predicting mammalian lifespan after controlling for extrinsic mortality, body mass, and metabolic rate (classics-p2).

**Proposed Experiments:**
- 2x2x2 Factorial Pathogen Challenge: Testing longevity interventions in SPF vs. rewilded environments with pre-registered endpoints for infection, reproduction, and cancer (docs/experiments/research_agenda.md#1-pathogen-challenge-factorial-2x2x2).
- Global Phylogenetic Comparative Analysis: Testing if population structure variables add predictive power for lifespan across 100+ species beyond standard allometric and extrinsic hazard metrics (classics-p2).

## Round 3 — CRITIC

### Final Assessment: The Adaptive Boundary of Pathogen Control

Classic Models (Medawar, Williams, Kirkwood) view aging as a failure of selection to maintain an individual. Pathogen Control (PC) argues this "failure" is actually a selection for group-level survival against epidemics. The defender’s Round 2 response relies on rebranding PC’s specific predictions as generic pleiotropic accidents, which we refute.

#### 1. Ultimate Level: Passive Shadow vs. Active Exclusion
Classics rely on the bridge assumption of **The Selection Shadow** (Hamilton 1966), where selection simply "runs out." PC proposes the bridge of **Inclusive Fitness Defense** ( @LidskyAndino2020): in a population with infectious disease, long-lived individuals become chronic pathogen reservoirs. The shadow is not a passive void but an active evolutionary boundary where death is selected to protect kin from reservoir-driven extinction. The "Flyers Paradox" is not just low extrinsic hazard; high dispersal (birds/bats) specifically breaks local transmission chains, reducing the selective pressure for early death ( @LidskyAndino2022).

#### 2. Proximate Level: Antagonistic Pleiotropy vs. Antiviral Militia
Classics rely on **Antagonistic Pleiotropy** (Williams 1957) as a generic early-benefit/late-cost tradeoff. PC proposes the bridge of **Functional Senescence**: the "Antiviral Militia" ( @LidskyAndino2022). If senescent cells are an evolved defense, their removal (senolytics) should show a specific **Pathogen-Environment Interaction** (pc-p1). If senolytics improve health in SPF but impair survival under viral challenge, it confirms senescence is a programmed defense, not just "damage" that selection forgot to fix.

#### 3. Measurement Level: Resource Tradeoffs vs. Epidemic Persistence
Classics rely on **Fitness Decoupling** (classics-p1), predicting generic costs for longevity. PC predicts that **Population Structure** (dispersal, cohort mixing) will be a superior predictor of lifespan because it dictates epidemic dynamics ( @LidskyAndino2020). The defender’s claim that this is just a correlate of extrinsic risk is a measurement gap that can only be resolved by the comparative test (pc-p2).

### The Decisive Test
Classics claim that SPF lifespan gains reflect the removal of "stresses." PC claims they reflect the removal of the **Primary Selective Pressure**. The only way to differentiate is the **2x2x2 Factorial Pathogen Challenge** (docs/experiments/research_agenda.md#1-pathogen-challenge-factorial-2x2x2). If longevity interventions fail to produce the predicted infection penalty in rewilded environments, Pathogen Control is falsified.

**Key Points:**
- The selection shadow is an active boundary driven by inclusive fitness in the presence of pathogens, not a passive void.
- Flyer longevity is driven by population dispersal breaking epidemic persistence, a variable distinct from simple extrinsic hazard.
- Cellular senescence is a functional antiviral adaptation (Antiviral Militia), making its clearance an environment-dependent risk.
- Lifespan gains in SPF environments are artifacts of removing the selective driver of the aging program.

**Criticisms:**
- Circular Allometry: Attributing flyer longevity to 'low risk' without explaining why dispersal tracks epidemic persistence better than body mass (S1-CLASSICS-PC-02).
- Active vs. Passive Shadow: Hamilton’s mathematical inevitability ignores the inclusive fitness penalty of being a chronic pathogen reservoir ( @LidskyAndino2020).
- Specific vs. Generic Tradeoffs: Classics' resource-based framing (classics-p1) cannot explain why longevity costs are specifically linked to host-defense rather than general metabolic failure (pc-p1).
- The SPF Blind Spot: Classics misinterpret the absence of pathogens as the absence of 'damage,' whereas PC identifies it as the removal of the adaptive setpoint driver ( @LidskyAndino2022).

**Falsifiers:**
- If longevity interventions (OSK or senolytics) in rewilded mice show no increase in pathogen burden or transmission (qPCR titers < 1.3x vehicle) across 3+ pathogen classes (pc-p1).
- If population structure variables (dispersal, contact density) provide < 2% incremental R-squared in predicting mammalian lifespan after controlling for extrinsic mortality and allometry (pc-p2).
- If a longevity intervention yielding >= 15% max lifespan gain shows no fitness costs in either SPF or rewilded environments (falsifies both Classics and PC).

**Proposed Experiments:**

### Winner: **Critic (Pathogen Control)**

The critic wins on Popperian grounds, but narrowly and with important caveats.

---

### Reasoning

#### 1. Falsifiability and Riskiness

Both theories now have operationalized predictions in `data/claims.yaml`. Season 3 operationalization resolved the prior definitional-escape criticism against Classics (S1-CLASSICS-PC-01). However, the asymmetry remains:

- **Classics (classics-p1)** predicts that *some* fitness cost must appear for any >= 15% lifespan gain. The disjunctive threshold (tumor OR reproduction OR stress resistance) is broad — three independent escape routes make it harder to falsify. This is a **measurement-level** claim with a **proximate-to-measurement bridge** (metabolic tradeoffs → observable costs).
- **PC (pc-p1)** predicts a *specific* infection penalty under dirty/rewilded conditions, with quantitative qPCR thresholds and a rescue arm. This is riskier because it commits to a single measurable domain (host defense) and a specific environmental interaction.
- **PC (pc-p2)** predicts population structure adds >= 5% incremental R² beyond extrinsic mortality. Classics (classics-p2) predicts < 2%. This is a clean, symmetric falsifier — one side must lose.

PC scores higher on riskiness (repo scores: PC=3, Classics=2) and novel predictions (PC=3, Classics=1). The debate did not change this.

#### 2. Surviving Severe Tests

Neither theory has survived a severe test *of the predictions contested in this debate*:
- Classics' foundational predictions (Gompertzian mortality, extrinsic-mortality correlates) are well-tested but are **not at stake** in this matchup. The contested predictions (classics-p1, classics-p2) are untested.
- PC has zero completed severe tests in the repo (all evidence is modeling/review: @LidskyAndino2020, @LidskyAndino2022).

This symmetry of untested predictions means severe-test history does not discriminate here.

#### 3. The Defender's Subsumption Argument

The defender's strongest move was arguing that PC's antiviral militia is simply a *specific instance* of antagonistic pleiotropy (Round 2). This is partially valid at the proximate level: senescent cells providing early-life pathogen defense at late-life cost is consistent with Williams (1957). However, this subsumption fails at the **ultimate level**: Classics predict that population structure should NOT independently predict lifespan after controlling for extrinsic mortality (classics-p2), while PC predicts it SHOULD (pc-p2). If pc-p2 is confirmed, the subsumption breaks — Classics would need to explain *why* structure matters, which their current framework does not naturally do (as conceded on the Classic Models theory page: "PC highlights lifespan differences that track population structure — patterns Classics do not naturally predict").

#### 4. The SPF Environment Critique

The critic's argument that SPF lifespan gains are artifacts of removing the selective driver (pathogens) is a **novel prediction** that Classics do not generate. Classics predict SPF gains reflect removal of generic stressors; PC predicts they specifically reflect removal of the infection-ecology driver, and that reintroducing pathogens should reveal an interaction invisible in SPF. This is a genuinely discriminating prediction at the measurement level.

#### 5. Why the Critic Wins

The critic wins because:
1. PC generates **novel, discriminating predictions** that Classics do not (population structure, environment-specific infection penalties).
2. PC's falsifiers are **more specific and riskier** than Classics' disjunctive tradeoff prediction.
3. The defender's subsumption argument ("PC is just pleiotropy") fails at the ultimate level where population structure is predicted to matter independently.
4. The measurement gap (S1-CLASSICS-PC-02) — Classics' inability to predict population-structure-linked lifespan patterns — remains unresolved and was not adequately addressed by the defender.

#### 6. Why the Win is Narrow

1. PC has **zero empirical severe tests** in the repo. Its advantage is theoretical riskiness, not empirical track record.
2. The inclusive-fitness bridge (S1-PC-CLASSICS-02) remains untested — the kin-benefit must exceed individual reproductive value loss, and this has only been modeled, not measured.
3. The defender correctly notes that dispersal and extrinsic mortality are likely correlated. Until the phylogenetic comparative analysis is completed, the population-structure claim is suggestive but not confirmed.

### Top 3 Surviving Criticisms

1. **Population-structure measurement gap (S1-CLASSICS-PC-02)**: Classics cannot predict population-structure-linked lifespan patterns (dispersal, eusociality, cohorting). The theory page itself concedes this. Until classics-p2 is tested, this remains an unresolved gap. *(Measurement-level)*

2. **Disjunctive tradeoff flexibility**: Classics-p1 predicts that *at least one* of three fitness costs must appear, giving three independent chances to avoid falsification. PC's pc-p1 predicts a specific infection penalty in a specific environment. Classics' prediction is less risky. *(Measurement-level)*

3. **SPF blind spot**: Classics interpret SPF lifespan gains as removal of generic stressors. PC interprets them as removal of the primary selective driver. Classics have no framework to predict which stressors matter most or why pathogen removal specifically enables large gains. *(Proximate-to-measurement bridge gap)*

### Top 3 Strongest Defenses

1. **Subsumption of antiviral militia under antagonistic pleiotropy**: The defender correctly argues that senescent cells providing early-life pathogen defense at late-life cost is formally consistent with Williams (1957). This neutralizes the proximate-level critique, forcing the debate to the ultimate and measurement levels where PC has the advantage. *(Proximate-level)*

2. **Hamilton's mathematical inevitability**: The selection shadow is a mathematical consequence of any population with non-zero extrinsic mortality (Hamilton 1966). It does not require pathogen-specific mechanisms and is therefore more parsimonious. The burden of proof is on PC to show that inclusive fitness adds explanatory power beyond this baseline. *(Ultimate-level)*

3. **PC's empirical deficit**: PC's in-repo evidence consists entirely of theoretical modeling and review articles (@LidskyAndino2020, @LidskyAndino2022). Classics rest on decades of cross-taxa empirical work. While the contested predictions are symmetric in their untested status, Classics' foundational framework is better empirically grounded. *(Meta-level)*

### Decisive Next Tests

1. **2×2×2 Factorial Pathogen Challenge** (docs/experiments/research_agenda.md#1-pathogen-challenge-factorial-2x2x2): Longevity intervention × environment (SPF vs rewilded) × anti-pathogen treatment. Primary endpoints: pathogen burden (qPCR), transmission proxies, fitness costs (tumors, reproduction, stress resistance). **Discriminates**: If infection penalty appears in dirty conditions and is rescued by anti-pathogen treatment → favors PC (pc-p1). If only generic fitness costs appear regardless of pathogen status → favors Classics (classics-p1). If no costs appear at all → falsifies both.

2. **Global Phylogenetic Comparative Analysis** (pc-p2 vs classics-p2): Population structure variables (dispersal, cohort mixing, contact networks) tested for incremental R² in predicting lifespan across >= 50 mammalian species, controlling for body mass, metabolic rate, and extrinsic mortality. **Discriminates**: >= 5% incremental R² → favors PC; < 2% → favors Classics.

3. **Senolytic pathogen-challenge arm**: Senolytics (D+Q) administered to rewilded mice with pre-registered infection endpoints (MHV, MNV, Helicobacter qPCR titers). If senescent-cell clearance specifically impairs host defense under infection (>= 2-fold increase in pathogen burden) → supports PC's antiviral militia; if clearance improves healthspan without infection penalty → supports Classics' damage-accumulation view.

4. **Inclusive fitness quantification**: Measure the actual reproductive value cost of early death versus kin-survival benefit of reducing pathogen reservoir lifespan in a controlled epidemic model (laboratory mice with tracked pedigrees and controlled pathogen introduction). **Discriminates**: If kin-survival benefit exceeds individual reproductive value loss under realistic parameters → PC's ultimate-level bridge is validated; if not → PC's adaptive death claim lacks evolutionary plausibility.
