# Factorial Senolytic × Bioelectric Intervention

## The Crucial Question

Does restoring bioelectric pattern control provide rejuvenation gains *on top of* damage repair, or is damage repair alone sufficient?

- **SENS Prediction:** Damage repair (e.g., senolytics) is rate-limiting. Bioelectric modulation will add little to nothing beyond what repair achieves.
- **Bioelectric Prediction:** The pattern-maintenance controller also degrades. Repair without controller restoration yields diminishing returns; the combination will be synergistic.

## Protocol

**Organism:** Naturally aged C57BL/6 mice, 20–22 months old (~60–70 human years).

**Design:** 4 arms, randomized, blinded, n ≥ 30 per arm.

| Arm | Damage Repair | Bioelectric Intervention |
|-----|--------------|------------------------|
| 1 | Vehicle control | Sham |
| 2 | Senolytic (dasatinib + quercetin) | Sham |
| 3 | Vehicle control | Ion channel modulation (e.g., ivermectin + kanamycin targeting GJC/Kir channels) |
| 4 | Senolytic | Ion channel modulation |

**Endpoints:**
1.  **Primary:** Maximum lifespan (top-decile survival).
2.  **Secondary:** Healthspan composite (grip, treadmill, maze).
3.  **Mechanism:**
    *   **Information-theoretic metrics:** Active information storage, transfer entropy (Bioelectric signature).
    *   **Senescent cell burden:** p16/SA-β-gal (SENS signature).

## Interpretation

- **Bioelectric Control wins if:** Arm 4 > Arm 2 ≈ Arm 3 > Arm 1. (Controller is independently rate-limiting).
- **SENS wins if:** Arm 2 > Arm 4 ≈ Arm 2 > Arm 3 ≈ Arm 1. (Damage repair captures all available gains).
- **Synthesis if:** Arm 4 >> Arm 2 and Arm 3. (Synergy implies both are partial drivers).
