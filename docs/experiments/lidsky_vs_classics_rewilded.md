# Lifespan Extension Under Controlled Pathogen Challenge

## The Crucial Question

Does extending lifespan under naturalistic conditions incur an obligatory infection penalty (higher pathogen persistence/transmission)?

-   **Pathogen Control Prediction:** Extending lifespan breaks the "epidemiological firewall," leading to higher pathogen burden and transmission to sentinels, erasing survival gains.
-   **Classic Models Prediction:** Longevity interventions generally improve health; any infection risk is specific to the intervention's mechanism (e.g., immunosuppression), not an inevitable consequence of living longer.

## Protocol

**Subjects:** Genetically identical inbred mice.
**Design:** 4 arms, "dirty mouse" (rewilded) housing (co-housed with pet-store mice).

| Arm | Housing | Intervention |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | SPF (Clean) | Rapamycin (Positive Control) |
| **2** | Rewilded (Dirty) | Rapamycin |
| **3** | Rewilded (Dirty) | Caloric Restriction (CR) (Immune-enhancing control) |
| **4** | Rewilded (Dirty) | Vehicle Control |

**Measurements:**
1.  **Lifespan:** Median and maximum.
2.  **Pathogen Burden:** Viral qPCR panel, metagenomic sequencing at death.
3.  **Transmission Rate:** Sentinel mice introduced at intervals; measure seroconversion.

## Interpretation

-   **Pathogen Control Wins if:** Arms 2 & 3 show higher pathogen burden/transmission than Arm 4, *and* lifespan gains are abolished or reversed.
-   **Classic Models Win if:** Arms 2 & 3 retain lifespan gains without systematic increases in pathogen burden/transmission (controlling for Rapamycin's specific immunosuppression via the CR arm).
