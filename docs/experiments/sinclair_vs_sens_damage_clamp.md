# Epigenetic Reset Under Clamped Damage Load

## The Crucial Question

Is the epigenetic reset genuinely upstream (capable of overcoming ongoing damage), or is it constrained by the damage load of the substrate?

-   **Epigenetic Prediction:** OSK resets the control layer, reactivating repair machinery and restoring function even against a headwind of damage (Group B ≈ Group A).
-   **SENS Prediction:** Damage is the binding constraint. If damage is high, the reset cannot hold or restore function (Group B << Group A). Damage removal alone (Group C) may match reset alone (Group A).

## Protocol

**Subjects:** Aged wild-type mice (18–20 months).
**Design:** 4 arms, randomized, blinded, n ≥ 20 per arm.

| Group | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **A** | Cyclic partial reprogramming (OSK) alone |
| **B** | OSK + continuous low-dose paraquat (sustained mitochondrial ROS) + injection of senescent cell–conditioned medium (sustained SASP) |
| **C** | Senolytics (ABT-263) + mitochondria-targeted antioxidant (MitoQ) alone (damage removal, no reset) |
| **D** | Vehicle control |

**Measurements (at 6-month endpoint):**
1.  **Epigenetic Clocks:** Horvath-style DNA methylation age.
2.  **Functional Assays:** Grip strength, treadmill endurance, Barnes maze.
3.  **Tissue Markers:** Senescent cell burden (p16/p21), mitochondrial function (respirometry), extracellular crosslinks.

## Interpretation

-   **Epigenetic Info Wins if:** Group B (OSK + Damage) shows substantial functional rejuvenation comparable to Group A. The reset overrides the damage signal.
-   **SENS Wins if:** Group B shows markedly attenuated benefit compared to Group A, or if Group C (Damage Removal) matches/exceeds Group A. Damage is the rate-limiting step.
-   **Synthesis if:** Groups A and C both outperform control but via different pathways, suggesting independent parallel drivers.
