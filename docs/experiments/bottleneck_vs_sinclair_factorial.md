# Factorial Bottleneck Suppression vs. Epigenetic Reprogramming

## The Crucial Question

Is epigenetic information loss causally upstream of damage-response pathways (so restoring information adds gains beyond pathway suppression), or are epigenetic changes a downstream consequence of pathway activation (so pathway suppression captures all available benefit)?

-   **Epigenetic Prediction:** OSK restores the upstream control layer, adding lifespan gains on top of bottleneck suppression (Group D > Group B). OSK alone may beat bottlenecks alone (Group C > Group B).
-   **Bottleneck Prediction:** Pathway suppression captures the causal drivers; epigenetic reset adds little/nothing or is redundant (Group B ≈ Group D). Bottlenecks alone are more potent/tractable (Group B > Group C).

## Protocol

**Subjects:** Genetically heterogeneous mice (UM-HET3), initiated at ~12 months.
**Design:** 4 arms, N≥60 per arm.

| Group | Bottleneck Intervention | Epigenetic Intervention |
|-------|------------------------|------------------------|
| **A** | Vehicle | Vehicle (control) |
| **B** | Rapamycin + Dasatinib/Quercetin (D+Q) | Vehicle |
| **C** | Vehicle | Dox-inducible OSK (cycled) |
| **D** | Rapamycin + D+Q | Dox-inducible OSK (cycled) |

**Measurements:**
1.  **Primary:** Median and maximum lifespan.
2.  **Secondary:** Epigenetic clocks, senescent cell burden, inflammatory markers (SASP), tumor incidence.

## Interpretation

-   **Epigenetic Info Wins if:** Group D significantly exceeds Group B (additivity implies upstream leverage), or Group C >> Group B.
-   **Bottleneck Wins if:** Group B ≈ Group D (redundancy implies pathways are the driver), or Group B >> Group C (pathways are the stronger lever).
