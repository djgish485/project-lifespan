# Epigenetic Clock Editing vs. Damage Accumulation

## The Crucial Question

Is the epigenetic clock an autonomous causal driver of aging (Programmatic) or a downstream readout of accumulated damage (Classic Models)?

-   **Programmatic Prediction:** Imposing an aged clock pattern drives functional aging even without damage. Freezing the clock prevents aging even with damage accumulation.
-   **Classic Models Prediction:** Imposing clock patterns is cosmetic and inert; aging requires actual damage. Freezing the clock won't stop aging because damage will accumulate anyway.

## Protocol

**Subjects:** Wild-type mice.
**Intervention:** dCas9-DNMT3A/TET1 epigenetic editing targeted to key clock CpG sites.

| Arm | Description |
|---|---|
| **1 (Acceleration)** | Impose aged methylation pattern on young mice *without* inducing DNA breaks/damage. |
| **2 (Deceleration)** | Maintain youthful methylation pattern in aging mice (freeze the clock). |
| **3 (Control)** | dCas9 with non-targeting guides (sham editing). |

**Measurements:**
1.  **Functional Aging:** Sarcopenia, immune decline, cognitive function.
2.  **Molecular Damage:** Oxidative lesions, crosslinks, somatic mutations (to confirm decoupling).
3.  **Epigenetic Age:** To confirm editing success.

## Interpretation

-   **Programmatic Wins if:** Arm 1 ages prematurely despite low damage; Arm 2 resists aging despite normal damage accumulation.
-   **Classic Models Win if:** Arm 1 remains healthy (clock is just a readout); Arm 2 ages normally (damage drives phenotype regardless of clock state).
